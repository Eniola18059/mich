1. Place Value (up to Millions)

What place value means

Every digit in a number has a value based on where it sits. Grade 4 works with numbers up to the millions place.

Number: 4,527,981
4 = 4,000,000 (millions) · 5 = 500,000 (hundred thousands) · 2 = 20,000 (ten thousands)
7 = 7,000 (thousands) · 9 = 900 (hundreds) · 8 = 80 (tens) · 1 = 1 (ones)

Expanded form

4,527,981 = 4,000,000 + 500,000 + 20,000 + 7,000 + 900 + 80 + 1

Reading and writing numbers in words

Split big numbers into 3-digit groups using the commas — each group is read like a small number, then you say its group name.

4,527,981 → "four million, five hundred twenty-seven thousand, nine hundred eighty-one"

Comparing numbers

Line digits up by place value, starting from the largest place. The first place where digits differ decides which number is bigger. If every digit matches, the numbers are equal.

Compare 356,201 and 356,198
3=3, 5=5, 6=6, 2 (0)0 vs 1(9)8 → compare the hundreds: 2 > 1
so 356,201 > 356,198

Ordering numbers

To put a list in order: compare the numbers with the most digits first (more digits usually means a bigger number), then use place-value comparison for numbers with the same digit count.

Order from least to greatest: 45,302 / 8,910 / 45,032 / 129,004
Step 1 — sort by digit count: 8,910 (4 digits) is smallest; 129,004 (6 digits) is biggest.
Step 2 — compare 45,302 vs 45,032: 4=4, 5=5, then 3>0 → 45,302 > 45,032
Answer: 8,910 < 45,032 < 45,302 < 129,004

Rounding

Rounding makes a number simpler while keeping it close to the original. Steps:

  1. Find the place you're rounding to and underline it.
  2. Look at the digit immediately to its right — this is the "decider" digit.
  3. Decider is 5, 6, 7, 8, or 9 → round up (add 1 to the underlined digit).
  4. Decider is 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4 → round down (keep the underlined digit the same).
  5. Change every digit to the right of the underlined place to 0.
Round 683,417 to the nearest thousand
Underline the thousands digit: 683,417 → decider is the hundreds digit (4)
4 is less than 5 → round down → 683,000
Round 2,758 to the nearest hundred
Underline the hundreds digit: 2,758 → decider is the tens digit (5)
5 rounds up → 700 becomes 800 → 2,800
💡 Trick: cover every digit to the right of your rounding place with your finger, then decide up or down using only the very next digit.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Mixing up "ten thousands" and "hundred thousands" — always count place columns from the ones digit.
  • Rounding using the wrong digit (always the one right next to the rounding place, never further away).
  • Forgetting to zero out the digits after rounding.

Try it yourself

1) What is the value of the 7 in 372,456?   2) Round 91,482 to the nearest ten thousand.   3) Order: 6,204 / 62,004 / 6,024 (least to greatest).

Answers: 1) 70,000   2) 90,000   3) 6,024 < 6,204 < 62,004

2. Addition & Subtraction

Adding multi-digit numbers

Line up numbers by place value (ones under ones, tens under tens, and so on). Add one column at a time starting from the ones column on the right. If a column's total is 10 or more, write down the ones digit and carry the tens digit to the top of the next column.

1 1  ← carried digits
  2 4 6 7
+ 1 8 9 5
———————
  4 3 6 2

Ones: 7+5=12 → write 2, carry 1
Tens: 6+9+1=16 → write 6, carry 1
Hundreds: 4+8+1=13 → write 3, carry 1
Thousands: 2+1+1=4

Subtracting multi-digit numbers

Line up the same way. Subtract one column at a time from the right. If the top digit is smaller than the bottom digit, borrow (regroup) 1 from the column to its left — that column's digit goes down by 1, and 10 is added to your current column.

Start:  5 0 0 3  −  1 8 7 6
Ones: 3 < 6, so borrow from tens → tens digit 0 becomes 9 (after its own borrow chain) and ones becomes 13 → 13−6 = 7
Tens: now 9, borrow again for hundreds → hundreds 0 becomes 9, tens becomes 9−1=8 → 8−7 = 1
Hundreds: now 9, borrow from thousands → thousands 5 becomes 4, hundreds becomes 9 → 9−8 = 1
Thousands: 4−1 = 3
Answer: 3,127

Regrouping across zeros

When you need to borrow but the next column is 0, you must "chain" the borrow further left until you reach a column with something to lend.

7,004 − 2,586
Ones column needs to borrow, but tens and hundreds are 0 — borrow ripples from the thousands: 7,004 becomes 6, 9, 9, 14 → 6,994 + borrowed ones
6,994+10−2,586... following the steps carefully gives 4,418

Estimating sums & differences

Round each number to its largest place first, then add/subtract the rounded numbers. This tells you if your exact answer is reasonable — a good habit for catching mistakes.

3,872 + 5,190 ≈ 4,000 + 5,000 = 9,000 (exact answer 9,062 — very close, so it's reasonable)

Word problems — step by step

Problem: A school had 4,215 books. It donated 1,348 to another school and later received 620 new books. How many books does it have now?
Step 1 (donated): 4,215 − 1,348 = 2,867
Step 2 (received): 2,867 + 620 = 3,487 books

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting to carry the extra ten into the next column.
  • Borrowing but forgetting to reduce the digit you borrowed from.
  • Not lining up place values correctly when the numbers have different digit counts.

3. Multiplication

Multiplying by a 1-digit number

Multiply each digit of the bigger number by the 1-digit number, working right to left, carrying extra tens just like in addition.

   4
  3 4 8
×    6
———————
2 0 8 8

Ones: 8×6=48 → write 8, carry 4
Tens: 4×6=24, +4 carried = 28 → write 8, carry 2
Hundreds: 3×6=18, +2 carried = 20 → write 20

Multiplying a 2-digit × 2-digit number (long multiplication)

Multiply the top number by the ones digit of the bottom number first (row 1). Then multiply the top number by the tens digit of the bottom number, shifting your answer one place left because you're really multiplying by a multiple of ten (row 2). Add the two rows.

  4 3
× 2 7
———————
  3 0 1  (43×7)
8 6 0    (43×20, shifted left)
———————
1,1 6 1

Area model / breaking numbers apart (distributive method)

Split one or both numbers into tens and ones, multiply each piece, then add all the partial products. This mirrors exactly what long multiplication does, step by step.

43 × 27 = 43 × (20 + 7) = (43×20) + (43×7) = 860 + 301 = 1,161
Breaking both apart: 43 × 27 = (40+3)(20+7)
= 40×20 + 40×7 + 3×20 + 3×7
= 800 + 280 + 60 + 21 = 1,161

Multiplying by multiples of 10 and 100

Multiply the non-zero digits first, then attach the same number of zeros.

60 × 300 → 6×3=18, then attach 1+2=3 zeros → 18,000

Estimating products

38 × 52 ≈ 40 × 50 = 2,000 (exact answer 1,976 — close, so it's reasonable)

Word problems

A theatre has 24 rows with 18 seats in each row. How many seats total?
24 × 18 = (24×10) + (24×8) = 240 + 192 = 432 seats

Multiplication facts to know cold

6×7=42
7×8=56
8×9=72
9×9=81
7×7=49
8×8=64
6×9=54
7×9=63

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting to shift the second row one place left in long multiplication.
  • Losing track of a carried digit.
  • Miscounting zeros when multiplying multiples of 10/100.

4. Long Division (with Remainders)

The steps: Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring down (DMSB)

1 5 r2
7 ⟌ 1 0 7
  − 7    (7×1)
  ——
   3 7
  −3 5   (7×5)
  ——
    2    ← remainder

1. Divide: how many times does 7 go into the first digit(s) of what's left?
2. Multiply: that answer × the divisor.
3. Subtract: the result from the part you're dividing.
4. Bring down the next digit and repeat from step 1 until there are no more digits.

A bigger example — 3-digit divided by 1-digit

    2 1 3
4 ⟌ 8 5 2
  −8    (4×2, using the 8)
  ——
    0 5
    −4   (4×1)
    ——
     1 2
    −1 2  (4×3)
      0    ← no remainder
Answer: 852 ÷ 4 = 213

What a remainder means

A remainder is what's left over after sharing as evenly as possible — it's always smaller than the divisor. Depending on the problem, you round the answer up, round it down, or report the remainder itself.

45 stickers packed into bags of 6 → 45÷6 = 7 r3 → you can fill 7 full bags, with 3 stickers left over (round DOWN)
45 students need vans that seat 6 each → 45÷6 = 7 r3 → you need 8 vans so nobody is left behind (round UP)

Checking your answer

divisor × quotient + remainder = dividend
7 × 15 + 2 = 107 ✓

Word problems

96 stickers shared equally among 8 friends → 96 ÷ 8 = 12 stickers each
A baker makes 134 muffins and puts 5 in each box. How many full boxes, and how many muffins are left over?
134 ÷ 5 = 26 r4 → 26 full boxes, 4 muffins left over

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Forgetting to bring down the next digit before dividing again.
  • Writing a remainder that's equal to or bigger than the divisor (it means you didn't divide enough times).
  • Placing digits in the wrong spot in the quotient (line them up above the digit you just brought down).

5. Factors, Multiples & Prime/Composite

Factors

Factors are numbers that divide evenly into another number (no remainder). Find them by testing each number starting from 1, in pairs.

Factors of 24: try 1×24, 2×12, 3×8, 4×6 (5 doesn't work) — so the factors are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24

Multiples

Multiples are what you get from skip-counting a number (multiplying it by 1, 2, 3…). A number has infinitely many multiples.

Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36 …

Greatest Common Factor (GCF)

The GCF of two numbers is the biggest number that divides evenly into both. List the factors of each number, then pick the largest one they share.

GCF of 18 and 24
Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18
Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24
Shared: 1, 2, 3, 6 → GCF = 6

Least Common Multiple (LCM)

The LCM of two numbers is the smallest number that's a multiple of both. List multiples of each until you find a match.

LCM of 4 and 6
Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16 …
Multiples of 6: 6, 12, 18 …
First match: 12

A prime number has exactly two factors: 1 and itself. It cannot be split evenly by any other number.

Composite numbers

A composite number has more than two factors (it can be divided evenly by numbers other than 1 and itself).

4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12 … are all composite.
💡 Note: 1 is neither prime nor composite — it only has one factor (itself).

Prime factorization

Every composite number can be broken down into a product of prime numbers only — its "prime building blocks." Keep dividing by the smallest prime that works until only primes are left.

Prime factorization of 60
60 ÷ 2 = 30 → 30 ÷ 2 = 15 → 15 ÷ 3 = 5 → 5 is prime
60 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 5

Word problems

Two runners lap a track every 4 and 6 minutes. If they start together, after how many minutes will they meet again at the start?
This is the LCM of 4 and 6 = 12 minutes
A baker wants to split 18 cookies and 24 brownies into identical goodie bags with no leftovers, using the most bags possible. How many bags?
This is the GCF of 18 and 24 = 6 bags (3 cookies + 4 brownies each)

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Mixing up GCF (biggest shared factor) with LCM (smallest shared multiple).
  • Forgetting 1 and the number itself when listing factors.
  • Calling 1 a prime number — it isn't (it only has one factor, not two).
